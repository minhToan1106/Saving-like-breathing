Beat Yourself: App Map

A take-home guide to where things live in the code, so you know where to look (or where to point an agent) when you want to change something.

What the app is

A morning bet against yourself ("Today I'll spend no more than $3") and a night check-in that decides Win or Lose. Wins build a streak and add to a monthly saving goal. The Diary holds your one balance. Everything runs in the browser, with no server, and is saved on this device.

The main pieces

  • index.html: the page, sidebar, and the order scripts load in.
  • js/home.js, js/diary.js, js/streaks.js: the three screens.
  • js/rules.js: all the money math, with no screen and no storage.
  • js/storage.js: the only file that saves or loads data.
  • js/catchup.js: on open, fills missed days as losses and resets savings in a new month.
  • js/achievements.js, js/theme.js, js/app.js: pop-ups, day/night, and startup plus navigation.
  • tests.html: checks the rules against the product's examples.

One action, end to end: tapping "Settle up"

The route you followed

Use Ctrl+F in your editor to search for the bold text. Line numbers can drift as the code changes; the names won't.

  1. Where the tap lands. js/home.js → search: function settle(night) Collects today's morning balance, income logged since the bet, and tonight's number. It also adds "Unlogged spending" or "Unlogged income" so the Diary matches real money. Just above it, the settleBtn click handler shows the "more than your Diary" warning first.
  2. The decision. js/rules.js → search: function settleDay The verdict formula: spent = morning + income − night (never below $0). Win if spent ≤ limit. Savings = the smaller of the limit and the suggested bet, minus spent. Pure math, which is why tests.html can check it.
  3. Where it's remembered. js/storage.js → search: function saveData The only place the app writes to localStorage. Moving to online storage later means rewriting this file.

A practice to reuse

Before approving an agent's design, ask it to walk through one concrete example with real numbers. Then keep that example as a test.

In this build, a $5 coffee before the bet and a $5 lunch after it were walked through before any code changed. That example showed two things: spending before the bet falls outside it, which the team accepted as a gap, and income logged before the bet would be counted twice. The same numbers now live in tests.html ("Coffee before the bet → morning $95; lunch after → $5 spent" and "Income logged before the bet is not counted twice"). The same habit caught the $100-bet savings loophole ("The cheat: bet $100, spend $5…").

On another project, the move is the same: ask for a worked example before saying yes, look for the case that feels wrong, and turn it into a check the agent has to pass.
