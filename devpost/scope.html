Beat Yourself (working title)

A web app where students bet on their own spending each morning and settle up each night, competing against their past selves instead of just logging numbers.

“Do you think you can beat yourself?”

The unique kernel

A daily bet against yourself, with stakes that sting. Commit in the morning, settle at night. Win and keep your streak plus an achievement. Lose and your streak resets to zero, and tomorrow's stakes get harder. The opponent is always “yesterday's you,” which keeps it fair for everyone, whatever their income.

The core loop: two visits a day

What “working” looks like: the 1-minute demo

Step through the demo video exactly as planned.

Who it's for

Students like us who run short of money by the end of the month, usually because of impulse buys. Typing numbers into a tracker “would be too boring,” and for many of them even just logging in is a big ask.

Why it matters to us

It's our own problem, and we think lots of people share it. As a team of two, we also want to learn “how to work with an AI agent.”

Inspiration & identity

CSS Battle: a normal website, clean and competitive, with one challenge front and center. The game energy of Valorant, TFT and Arena of Valor, and instant dopamine on a win. It should leave users salty, curious or thrilled. The word is “stakes,” never “gambling.”

The boundary

✅ Now: in the proof of concept
  • Morning check-in: choose the bet type (“spend no more than $X” or “end with at least $Y”), tap a stake amount, enter the balance.
  • Night check-in: enter the balance, get a win/lose verdict from the balance check.
  • Win → streak and achievement. Lose → streak resets, and the next day's stakes get harder.
  • A “Skip to tonight” demo button so a whole day fits in the video.
  • One user on one device. Nothing social.
⏳ Later: worth doing, not now
  • Real 10 PM notifications (browser notifications are tricky, and the demo can't wait until 10 PM).
  • Leaderboards and rankings among users.
  • Connecting with friends and referrals.
  • Exercise penalty (pushups) for losses.
  • A bigger achievement collection, with bad achievements to earn back.
✂️ Cut, with reasons
  • Charging $1 per failure: it punishes students who are already short on money, and the app would profit when users fail.
  • Gambling and addiction framing: an ethical and legal red flag for this audience. We kept the stakes and dropped the gambling.
  • Cheat-proof verification (bank connections): far beyond a 2–4 hour build. It runs on the honor system, and cheating only means losing to yourself.
